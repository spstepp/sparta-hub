Catholic Funeral Mass Program: Order of Service to Print

A funeral Mass program follows the order of the Mass: Introductory Rites, Liturgy of the Word, Liturgy of the Eucharist and Final Commendation. Add the names for each part, and confirm the final order, readings and music with the parish.

Introductory Rites

  • Greeting and sprinkling with holy water
  • Placing of the pall (and any Christian symbols)
  • Entrance procession and opening song
  • Opening prayer

Liturgy of the Word

  • First reading, usually from the Old Testament (reader’s name)
  • Responsorial psalm (cantor or choir)
  • Second reading, from the New Testament (reader’s name)
  • Gospel acclamation and Gospel (read by the priest or deacon)
  • Homily
  • Prayer of the faithful (intentions are often read by family members)

Liturgy of the Eucharist

  • Preparation of the gifts (gift bearers’ names)
  • Eucharistic prayer
  • Communion and Communion song

Final Commendation

  • Invitation to prayer and silence
  • Song of farewell
  • Prayer of commendation
  • Procession and closing song

Words of remembrance and eulogies

Many parishes keep a eulogy out of the Mass itself or allow one short remembrance by a family member, often before the final commendation. Others suggest saving longer stories for the vigil, the graveside or the reception. Ask the parish what they allow before you print.

Also worth listing

  • Celebrant (priest or deacon), readers, cantor, organist, gift bearers and pallbearers
  • Burial or committal location
  • Reception details

Quick answers

What is the order of a Catholic funeral Mass?

Introductory Rites (greeting, sprinkling, placing of the pall, opening prayer), Liturgy of the Word (readings, psalm, Gospel, homily, prayer of the faithful), Liturgy of the Eucharist (gifts, Eucharistic prayer, Communion), then the Final Commendation and procession. The parish confirms the details.

Can you give a eulogy at a Catholic funeral Mass?

It depends on the parish. Many allow one short remembrance, often before the final commendation, and suggest longer eulogies at the vigil, graveside or reception. Ask the parish first.
