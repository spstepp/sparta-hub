What to Write in a Funeral Program for Grandma

A funeral program for Grandma has the usual parts: her photo, name and dates on the cover, the order of service, a short life story, a verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family. Grandchildren often help with the life story and the small touches, and that is what makes it hers.

What goes inside

  1. Cover: a photo that looks like her, her full name (add the name the grandkids used, like “Nana” or “Grams”) and her dates.
  2. Order of service: music, readings, the eulogy and who gives each part.
  3. Her story: 100 to 200 words on where she grew up, her marriage, her work, her faith and what she loved.
  4. A verse or poem: Proverbs 31:28, a favorite hymn or a short poem.
  5. Thank-you: a few lines from the family to everyone who came.

Sample life story

Margaret “Nana” Lewis was born in 1936 on a farm outside Wichita, Kansas. She married Walter in 1957 and taught second grade for 31 years. She never missed Sunday dinner, kept a jar of lemon drops on the counter, and could finish the crossword in pen. She is survived by her three children, eight grandchildren and five great-grandchildren, who all know her pie crust recipe by heart.

Ways grandchildren can help

  • Each grandchild writes one line: “Nana taught me...”
  • A grandchild reads the poem or verse at the service
  • Grandchildren serve as honorary pallbearers, listed on the back panel
  • A small childhood photo with her on the back panel

Sample thank-you

Thank you for coming to remember Mom and Nana. Her table always had room for one more, and today it was full. Please join us after the service.

Quick answers

What do you write in a funeral program for a grandmother?

Her photo, full name and dates, the order of service, a short life story, a verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family. Many add the name the grandchildren called her.

Can grandchildren be pallbearers or honorary pallbearers?

Yes. Adult grandchildren often carry the casket, and younger ones are often listed as honorary pallbearers.

What is a good verse for a grandmother's funeral program?

Proverbs 31:28, “Her children arise up, and call her blessed,” is a common choice, as are Psalm 23 and a favorite hymn.
