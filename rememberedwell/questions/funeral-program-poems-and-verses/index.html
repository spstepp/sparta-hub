Short Poems and Verses for a Funeral Program

A short poem or verse on the back of the program gives people something to hold on to. Pick one that sounds like the person.

How to choose

  • Keep it short: 4 to 12 lines fits a panel without shrinking the type
  • Match the tone: gentle, hopeful, humorous or faith-based
  • Choose something the family can read aloud without stumbling

Where to look

  • Their own favorites: a song lyric, a line from a card or letter
  • Scripture such as Psalm 23 or Ecclesiastes 3, for a faith-based service
  • Older poems in the public domain, so you can print them freely. Check a poem’s copyright before using a modern one

How to lay it out

  • Center the text on the back panel, with the author’s name under it
  • Use a clear, larger font so older guests can read it
  • Leave white space around the words

Quick answers

Can I use a modern poem in a funeral program?

Many modern poems are copyrighted. For a small family program, check the author’s terms or choose a public-domain poem or scripture.

Where does the poem go in a funeral program?

Most families put it on the back panel, or beside the obituary on an inside panel.
