What to Write in a Funeral Program for Grandpa

A funeral program for Grandpa has the usual parts: his photo, name and dates on the cover, the order of service, a short life story, a verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family. The details that make it his are the name the grandkids called him, his work, his service if he served, and the things he was known for.

What goes inside

  1. Cover: a photo that looks like him, his full name (add “Grandpa,” “Papa” or “Pop” if the family used it) and his dates.
  2. Order of service: music, readings, the eulogy and who gives each part.
  3. His story: 100 to 200 words on where he grew up, his marriage, his work, any military service and what he loved.
  4. A verse or poem: 2 Timothy 4:7, Psalm 23 or a short poem.
  5. Thank-you: a few lines from the family to everyone who came.

Sample life story

Robert “Papa” Hayes was born in 1934 in Dayton, Ohio. He served in the Army in Korea, married June in 1958 and ran a hardware store on Main Street for 40 years. He could fix anything with duct tape and a pocketknife, never missed a grandchild’s ball game, and told the same three jokes at every holiday. He is survived by June, his four children, eleven grandchildren and six great-grandchildren.

Ways grandchildren can help

  • Each grandchild writes one line: “Papa taught me...”
  • A grandchild reads the verse or poem at the service
  • Grandchildren serve as pallbearers or honorary pallbearers, listed on the back panel
  • A photo of him with the grandkids on the back panel

If he served

Add his branch and years of service under his name or in the life story, such as “U.S. Army, 1952–1954.” Families often add the flag presentation and taps to the order of service. Our military funeral program page has more on this.

Sample thank-you

Thank you for being here to remember Dad and Papa. He always said the best part of any day was the people in it. Please join us after the service.

Quick answers

What do you write in a funeral program for a grandfather?

His photo, full name and dates, the order of service, a short life story, a verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family. Many add the name the grandchildren called him and his military service if he served.

How long should Grandpa's life story be in the program?

About 100 to 200 words. Pick a few details that sound like him rather than listing every job and place.

What is a good verse for a grandfather's funeral program?

2 Timothy 4:7, “I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith,” is a common choice, as are Psalm 23 and John 14:1-2.
