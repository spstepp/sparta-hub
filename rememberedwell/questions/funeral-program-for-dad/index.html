What to Write in a Funeral Program for Dad

A funeral program for Dad has the same parts as any program: his photo, name and dates on the cover, the order of service, a short life story, a verse that fits him, and a thank-you from the family. The life story is where he shows up, so spend your time there.

What goes inside

  1. Cover: a photo that looks like him, his full name (with the nickname people used) and his dates.
  2. Order of service: music, readings, the eulogy and who gives each part.
  3. His story: 100 to 200 words on where he grew up, his work or service, the family he raised and what he loved to do.
  4. A verse or poem: a hymn, scripture or short poem he would have liked.
  5. Thank-you: a few lines from the family to the people who came.

Sample life story

Robert "Bob" Harmon was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1948. He served two years in the Army, then spent 38 years as a machinist and never missed one of his kids' ballgames. He married Carol in 1971. Bob could fix anything with a lawn mower engine, kept the best tomato garden on the block, and told the same five jokes for fifty years. He is survived by Carol, his children Dan and Lisa, and six grandchildren.

Small touches families use

  • A photo of him at work, in uniform or with his fishing pole on the back panel
  • A saying he repeated, used as a heading
  • A short list: "Dad taught us..." with three or four things
  • For a veteran, his branch and years of service under his dates

Sample thank-you

Thank you for being here for Dad. He never liked a fuss, but he would have been glad to see every one of you. Please join us after the service for lunch and a few of his stories.

Quick answers

What do you write in a funeral program for your father?

His photo, full name and dates, the order of service, a short life story, a favorite verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family.

How long should Dad's life story be in the program?

100 to 200 words fits one panel: where he was born, his work or service, his marriage and children, what he loved, and who survives him.

What is a good verse for a father's funeral program?

Families often use Psalm 23, a favorite hymn, or a short poem such as Crossing the Bar. Pick one that sounds like him.
