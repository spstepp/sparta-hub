Funeral Program Cover Wording: Titles and Phrases for the Front

The front of a funeral program holds four things: a short title line, the person's full name, their birth and death dates, and a photo. The title line sets the tone, so pick one that fits the service.

Title lines that work

  • Traditional: In Loving Memory, In Memory Of, Funeral Service For
  • Celebration of life: Celebrating the Life of, A Life Well Lived, A Celebration of Life
  • Faith: Going Home, Absent from the Body, Present with the Lord, Mass of Christian Burial
  • Gentle and personal: Forever in Our Hearts, Always Remembered, Until We Meet Again

How to write the name and dates

Use the full name the family used, with a nickname in quotes if everyone knew them by it: Margaret "Peggy" Ellis. Dates can be full (June 4, 1941 to March 2, 2026) or short (1941 to 2026). Sunrise and Sunset are a softer way to label them.

Optional extras

  • The date, time and place of the service, in small type under the dates
  • A one-line verse or quote
  • A small cross, dove or flower if it suits the family

Keep the cover clean

The cover is read from across a room. One title line, a large name and a clear photo work better than a crowded front. Put the verse, biography and order of service inside.

Quick answers

What do you write on the front of a funeral program?

A title line such as In Loving Memory, the full name, the birth and death dates, and a photo. Some families add the service date and place in small type.

What can I say instead of In Loving Memory?

Celebrating the Life of, Forever in Our Hearts, A Life Well Lived, Always Remembered, or Going Home for a faith service.

Should the funeral program cover include a nickname?

Yes, if family and friends knew them by it. Put it in quotes between the first and last name.
