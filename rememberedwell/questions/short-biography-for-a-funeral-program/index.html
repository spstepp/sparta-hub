How to Write a Short Biography for a Funeral Program

A short biography for a funeral program runs about 100 to 200 words. Start with their name and where they were born, move through family, work and what they loved, and end with who they leave behind. Write it the way you would tell a friend about them.

A simple order to follow

  1. Who they were: full name, birthplace, and the years they lived.
  2. Family and home: where they grew up, who they married, where they raised their family.
  3. Work and service: a line or two about their job, church, military service or volunteering.
  4. What they loved: the garden, the lake, Sunday dinners, a team, a song.
  5. Who they leave behind: spouse, children and grandchildren by name, if space allows.

A sample you can adapt

Margaret Ellis was born in Dayton, Ohio, on June 4, 1941. She married Tom Ellis in 1963 and they raised three children in the house on Maple Street. Margaret taught second grade for thirty-one years and never met a child she could not make laugh. She baked for every church supper, kept the best tomato garden on the block, and loved a long phone call. She is survived by her children, Anne, Paul and Beth, and seven grandchildren who called her Nana.

Keep it short enough to fit

A folded program has room for roughly 150 words in a readable size. If you have more to say, put the full story in the eulogy or a longer obituary, and keep the program to the highlights.

What to leave out

Skip the cause of death unless the family wants it there, long lists of every relative, and home addresses. Check dates and name spellings with another family member before printing.

Quick answers

How long should a funeral program biography be?

About 100 to 200 words. That fits a folded program in a readable size. Save longer stories for the eulogy.

What do you include in a short biography for a funeral?

Full name, birth date and place, family, work or service, what they loved, and who survives them.

Is a funeral program biography the same as an obituary?

No. An obituary is a public notice, often in a newspaper or online. The program biography is a shorter, warmer note for the people at the service.
