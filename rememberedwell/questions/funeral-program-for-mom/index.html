What to Write in a Funeral Program for Mom

A funeral program for Mom has the same parts as any program: her photo, name and dates on the cover, the order of service, a short life story, a verse she loved, and a thank-you from the family. What makes it hers is the detail you put in the life story.

What goes inside

  1. Cover: a photo that looks like her on a good day, her full name and her dates.
  2. Order of service: music, readings, the eulogy and who gives each part.
  3. Her story: 100 to 200 words on where she grew up, the family she raised, her work, and what she loved.
  4. A verse or poem: a favorite hymn, scripture or a short poem.
  5. Thank-you: a few lines from the family to the people who came.

Sample life story

Linda Carter was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, in 1952, the oldest of four. She married Jim Carter in 1974 and raised three children who always had a packed lunch and a note inside it. Linda worked as a nurse for 35 years and was the first person neighbors called when someone got hurt. She loved her rose bushes, Saturday garage sales, and every one of her eight grandchildren. She is survived by Jim, her children Amy, Mark and Kate, and her grandchildren.

Small touches families use

  • Her own handwriting, scanned from a card or recipe, on the back panel
  • A line she always said, used as a heading
  • A short list: "Mom taught us..." with three or four things

Sample thank-you

Thank you for loving our mother and for being here today. Your cards, meals and prayers have carried us. She would have wanted everyone to stay for coffee.

Quick answers

What do you write in a funeral program for your mother?

Her photo, full name and dates, the order of service, a short life story, a favorite verse or poem, and a thank-you from the family.

How do you write a short obituary for Mom for the program?

Keep it to 100 to 200 words: where she was born, who she married, the family she raised, her work, what she loved, and who survives her.

What is a good poem for a mother's funeral program?

Many families use a favorite hymn or scripture. Short poems such as Do Not Stand at My Grave and Weep fit a single panel.
