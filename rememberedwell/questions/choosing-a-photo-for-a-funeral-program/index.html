How to Choose the Photo for a Funeral Program

Choose a clear photo where they look like themselves and their face is well lit, ideally from the last 10 years or from a time the family remembers well. Use the original photo file, not a screenshot or a social media copy, so it prints sharp.

What makes a good cover photo

  • It looks like them: the smile, the glasses, the hat people knew. A candid shot often beats a formal portrait.
  • Face in focus and well lit: daylight from the front is best. Avoid photos where the face is in shadow.
  • Room to crop: a photo from the chest up crops cleanly into a portrait or oval frame.
  • Nothing distracting: other people can be cropped out, but busy backgrounds are hard to fix.

Making sure it prints sharp

For a cover photo about 4 by 5 inches, aim for at least 1200 by 1500 pixels. Most phone photos are far bigger than that. Ask family to send the original from their phone or email it as a full-size file; photos saved from Facebook or a text message are often shrunk and print blurry. An old print can be scanned at 300 to 600 dpi, or photographed flat in daylight.

Swapping it into a template

In Google Docs, click the sample photo, then right-click and pick Replace image. In Word, right-click the photo and pick Change Picture. The new photo takes the place of the old one, so the layout stays the same.

More than one photo

Put the main photo on the cover. A second photo, such as a younger picture or one with family, can go on the inside left or the back panel. Three or four photos is plenty for a folded program; save the rest for a slideshow or a photo board.

Quick answers

What kind of picture should go on a funeral program?

A clear photo where the person looks like themselves, with their face well lit and in focus. Many families pick a favorite candid photo over a formal portrait.

What size should a photo be for a funeral program?

For a cover photo about 4 by 5 inches, use at least 1200 by 1500 pixels. Most original phone photos are plenty big.

Can I use a photo from Facebook for a funeral program?

You can, but it may print blurry because Facebook shrinks photos. Ask whoever took it for the original file if you can.
