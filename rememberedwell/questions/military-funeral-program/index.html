Military Funeral Program: What to Include for a Veteran

A military funeral program follows the usual order of service and adds the veteran's branch, rank and years of service, plus a short section for the military honors: the folding of the flag, Taps, a rifle volley if there is one, and the presentation of the flag to the family.

What to add for a veteran

  • Name with rank and branch: for example “Staff Sergeant Robert L. Hayes, United States Army.”
  • Years of service: a line such as “Served 1968–1972, Vietnam” under the dates of birth and death.
  • A service emblem or flag: many families put a small flag or the branch emblem on the cover.
  • Military honors: list them in the order of service so guests know what is coming.
  • Honor guard and pallbearers: name the family pallbearers, and add a line for the honor guard or veterans group if one is taking part.

Where the honors go in the order

Honors usually come at the end, often at the graveside: the folding of the flag, Taps, a rifle volley if the honor guard offers one, then the flag presented to the next of kin. In the program, list them as plain lines, such as “Military Honors ........ U.S. Army Honor Guard.”

How to arrange military honors

Eligible veterans can receive military funeral honors at no cost to the family. At least two uniformed service members fold and present the flag, and Taps is played. The funeral director usually requests the honors for you, so ask them early and have the veteran's discharge papers (often the DD Form 214) ready.

A short verse that fits

Families often pick a hymn tied to the branch, such as the Navy Hymn, or a short line such as “Well done, good and faithful servant.” Keep it to a few lines so it fits the back panel.

Quick answers

What do you put in a funeral program for a veteran?

Their rank, branch and years of service, a flag or service emblem, and the military honors listed in the order of service, along with the usual welcome, readings, eulogy and closing.

When are military honors done at a funeral?

Usually at the end of the service or at the graveside: the flag is folded, Taps is played, a rifle volley may follow, and the flag is presented to the family.

Who sets up military funeral honors?

The funeral director usually requests them for the family. Have the veteran's discharge papers, often the DD Form 214, ready.
