Program for a Memorial Service After Cremation

A program for a memorial service after cremation looks just like a funeral program: a photo and dates on the cover, an order of service inside, and a thank-you on the back. The differences are small. There is usually no casket or burial, and the service may be held weeks later.

What changes, and what stays the same

  • Stays the same: cover photo, name and dates, order of service, readings, a short biography, and the family thank-you.
  • Leave out: pallbearers and burial or committal details, unless there will be a scattering or interment.
  • Add if it applies: a line about an urn or photo display, and where guests are invited afterward.

A simple order for a memorial service

  1. Music as guests gather
  2. Welcome and opening words
  3. Reading or prayer
  4. Life story or eulogy
  5. Memories shared by family and friends
  6. Closing words and a song
  7. Invitation to the reception

Wording for the date and place

If the service is later than usual, put the date on the cover plainly: “A Memorial Service for Robert Hayes, Saturday, June 7, 2025, at 2 p.m.” Add the address on the back so guests do not have to look it up.

Printing

Count on one program per household plus a few extras. Memorial services are often smaller, but people travel in, so print a little over your guest list.

Quick answers

Do you need a program for a memorial service?

It is not required, but guests appreciate one. It gives the order of the service, the names of speakers, and something to keep.

What is the difference between a memorial program and a funeral program?

The layout is the same. A memorial program usually leaves out pallbearers and burial details and may add where guests are invited afterward.

What do you put on a memorial service program?

A photo, the name and dates, the order of service, a short biography or verse, and a thank-you from the family.
