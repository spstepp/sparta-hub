What to Write in a Funeral Program, Panel by Panel

Most funeral programs are one sheet of US Letter paper folded in half into four panels: the cover, the life story, the order of service and the back panel.

Cover

  • A photo of your loved one
  • A heading such as “In Loving Memory” or “Celebrating the Life of”
  • Full name, with a nickname if everyone used it
  • Birth and death dates
  • Date, time and place of the service

Inside left: life story or obituary

  • Where and when they were born, and who raised them
  • Marriage, children and the people closest to them
  • Work, service, faith and community
  • What they loved: hobbies, places, sayings, small habits people will recognize
  • Survived by, and preceded in death by

Short and warm beats long and complete. If it doesn’t fit the panel, cut dates and lists before you cut stories.

Inside right: order of service

List each part in the order it happens, with the name of the person leading it. A common outline: prelude music, welcome, opening prayer or words, reading, music, eulogy, remarks from family or friends, closing prayer or words, recessional. Ask the officiant or funeral home for the final order before you print.

Back panel

  • A poem, verse or favorite quote
  • Pallbearers and honorary pallbearers
  • A short thank-you from the family
  • Burial, reception or memorial gift details

Before you print

  • Have two people check every name and date
  • Print one test copy, fold it, and make sure the cover is on the front
  • Print double-sided, “flip on short edge”, at 100% or Actual Size

Quick answers

What is a funeral program called?

It goes by several names: funeral program, order of service, memorial program or funeral bulletin. They all mean the printed handout guests receive at the service.

What goes in a funeral program?

Usually a cover with the photo, name and dates; the life story or obituary; the order of service; and a back panel with a poem or verse, pallbearers, a family thank-you and reception details.
